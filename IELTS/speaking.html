What the examiner is scoring

Fluency and coherence

Can you keep going, and do your ideas connect? Hesitating to find an idea is normal. Hesitating to find a word, repeatedly, is what costs marks.

The rule that matters most: if a sentence goes wrong, finish it anyway. A flawed sentence delivered smoothly beats a correct one restarted three times.

Lexical resource

Range and precision. Not long words — the right word, and not the same word eight times.

Do this: paraphrase yourself. If you say "important", follow it later with "essential" or "it really matters". Examiners hear the range.

Grammatical range and accuracy

A mix of tenses and structures. Simple sentences only, however accurate, caps you around 5.

Do this: get a past tense, a conditional and a present perfect into every long answer. Part 2 gives you room for all three.

Pronunciation

Being understood without effort. An accent is fine — every speaker has one. Word stress in the wrong place is the thing that breaks understanding.

Do this: slow down slightly. Speed is the commonest cause of a low pronunciation score, not accent.

Three things people get wrong

Answering Part 1 in two words

"Do you like your hometown?" — "Yes." That answer alone caps you at 5.5 regardless of your English.

Do this: answer, then add one sentence. Always. Answer plus reason, or answer plus example.

Stopping early in Part 2

Most candidates stop between 45 and 70 seconds and think they filled the time. The examiner stops you at two minutes — let them.

Do this: use all four bullets. The third one, the story, is what fills the middle minute.

Memorised answers

Examiners are trained to hear them, and they are marked down. Part 3 questions are unpredictable on purpose.

Do this: memorise flexible phrases, not topics. Frames like "I can see why people say that, but…" fit any subject.
Part 3 is where band 7 is won or lost. Part 1 and 2 are about you; Part 3 is abstract — society, government, the future. Opinion, reason, example, then a concession. Same shape every time.

Record yourself on your phone and run all three parts without stopping. One take.
