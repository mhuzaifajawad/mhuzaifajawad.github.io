On paper there is no transfer time in Reading, unlike Listening. Whatever is on your answer sheet when time is called is your test. Write answers straight onto the sheet as you go.

The four rules that decide your score

1. Twenty minutes per passage. No exceptions.

Passage 3 is the hardest and carries the same marks as passage 1. Students who overrun on passage 1 lose easy marks at the end that they would certainly have got.

Do this: when 20 minutes is up, move on even if two questions are blank. Guess them. There is no penalty for a wrong answer, so never leave a gap.

2. Do not read the passage first.

Read the questions, then hunt. Most question types follow the order of the passage, so answer 4 sits between answers 3 and 5. Reading the whole text first, then reading it again for each question, is doing the work twice.

Do this: skim the paragraph openings for 90 seconds to learn the shape of the text, then go to the questions.

3. The answer is in the passage, not in your head.

Every answer can be pointed at. If you are choosing based on what seems sensible, or what you already know about the topic, you are guessing.

Do this: underline the words in the text that gave you the answer. If you cannot underline anything, you have not found it yet.

4. Spelling counts, and so does the word limit.

A correctly located answer spelled wrong scores zero. So does a three-word answer where two were allowed.

Do this: copy the word exactly as it appears. Never change its form — if the passage says trees, do not write tree.

The three question types in this test

Matching headings

You get more headings than paragraphs, so some are never used. They are designed to look right.

Method: read the paragraph, cover the headings, say the main point in your own words, then find the heading closest to that. A heading matching one detail in the paragraph is usually the trap; the answer matches the whole paragraph.

True / False / Not Given

The type that costs the most marks. The distinction people get wrong is False versus Not Given.

False = the passage says the opposite. Not Given = the passage does not say. If you are arguing from what is likely or reasonable, the answer is Not Given. Never use outside knowledge.

Sentence completion

Words come straight from the passage. You are not paraphrasing.

Method: decide what part of speech is missing before you look. Then obey the limit — no more than two words means two is allowed, three is zero.
