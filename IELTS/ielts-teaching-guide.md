# Teaching guide — six days to the test

For you, not the student. Written for 60-minute sessions from 6 to 10 September.

---

## The principle for a sprint this short

You are not raising his English. There is no version of six days that does that. You are
removing the things that cost marks he has already earned: a missing overview, an answer that
stops at seventy seconds, a Task 2 that never states a position, twenty minutes lost on
passage one.

That reframing matters for how you teach. **Do not correct everything.** A student who leaves
with three fixed habits scores higher than one who leaves with a list of forty corrections he
cannot hold in his head. Pick three per session. Repeat them until he is sick of them.

Tell him the realistic target on day one. 6.0 to 6.5 is a good outcome from a 6.0 baseline
taken two years ago. If you let him believe 8 is coming, the lessons become about hope rather
than work, and he will blame the teaching for the result.

---

## The shape of every session

The same four blocks, every time. Predictability lets him relax into the work instead of
wondering what is coming.

| Minutes | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Homework check** | You look at what he sent. Two sentences of feedback, no more. |
| 5–15 | **One teaching point** | The single thing today is about. You explain, he asks. |
| 15–45 | **He works** | He does the task on the relevant page while you watch. You do not help. |
| 45–55 | **Marking together** | You go through the result. He says what he'd change. |
| 55–60 | **Set homework** | Specific, small, and sendable on WhatsApp. |

The block that matters most is 15–45, and the hard part is your silence. The moment you help
mid-task you have taught him he will have help in the exam.

---

## Day 1 — Saturday 6 September · Diagnostic

**Aim:** find out what he can actually do. Teach almost nothing.

- **0–20** Reading Room. He reads the brief himself while you watch the clock, then does the
  13-question test. Do not discuss it first.
- **20–45** Writing Desk, Task 2, 25 minutes rather than 40. You want the sample and the
  pressure, not a finished essay.
- **45–55** Speaking Room, Part 1 and one Part 2. Record it.
- **55–60** Tell him the three things you saw. Not more than three.

**Afterwards:** run his Task 2 through the Writing Marker. That gives you his real starting
bands and the priority list you will teach from all week.

**Homework:** one Task 1 from the Writing Desk, 20 minutes, sent as a photo.

**What you are looking for:** does he state a position? Does he reach 250 words? How long did
he speak in Part 2? Which reading question type collapsed? Write the four numbers down; you
will show them to him again on Day 5.

---

## Day 2 — Sunday 7 September · Task 1 and the overview

**Aim:** he leaves able to write an overview without being reminded.

- **Teaching point:** a Task 1 script with no overview is capped at 5 for Task Achievement no
  matter how good the English is. This is the highest-value ten minutes of the week.
- Mark his homework Task 1 together on the page. Nine times out of ten the overview is missing
  or contains numbers, which it should not.
- Show the model answer on the Writing Desk. Point at paragraph two and its label. Have him
  read the overview aloud and say what makes it an overview rather than a body paragraph.
- **He works:** a second Task 1, 20 minutes, timed. Overview must be paragraph two.

**Homework:** rewrite the first Task 1 with a proper overview. Same data, better shape.

**If he resists the formula** — some students find it mechanical — tell him the truth: Task 1
is the one part of the exam that rewards a formula, because the examiner is checking whether
you selected the main features. Save the creativity for Task 2.

---

## Day 3 — Monday 8 September · Part 2 intensive

**Aim:** two minutes, every time, without prompting.

- **Teaching point:** most candidates stop between 45 and 70 seconds and believe they filled
  the time. Show him his Day 1 recording length. That number does the teaching for you.
- Six cue cards back to back on the Speaking Room, hitting **New topic set** between them.
  Two minutes each, no coaching, no restarts. This is exhausting and that is the point.
- After the third one, show him the Part 2 model and the Part 1 weak-versus-strong pair.
  The contrast lands faster than any explanation of the criteria.
- Last two cards: he must use three phrases from the phrase bank in each.

**Homework:** two Part 2 recordings on his phone, different cards, sent to you. He listens to
each one once before sending.

**The rule to hammer:** if a sentence goes wrong, finish it. A flawed sentence delivered
smoothly beats a correct one restarted three times. Say it every time he restarts.

---

## Day 4 — Tuesday 9 September · Task 2

**Aim:** a clear position in the introduction, and one developed idea per paragraph.

- **Teaching point:** without a position stated up front, Task Response is capped at 5.
  Second point: a body paragraph with no *because* and no *for example* is a claim, not an
  argument.
- Take the three priorities the Writing Marker gave you on Day 1. Teach those, nothing else.
- **He works:** one full Task 2, 40 minutes, properly timed. This is the only full-length
  timed essay of the week, so protect it — no interruptions.
- Mark it with him on the page, then show the model and its paragraph labels.

**Homework:** none. The essay was enough. Tell him that explicitly, or he will invent work
and arrive tired.

---

## Day 5 — Wednesday 10 September · Remeasure and taper

**Aim:** show him he has moved, and settle him down.

- Full Speaking mock, all three parts, new topic set. Mark it on the four sliders while he
  watches you do it.
- Put Day 1's four numbers next to today's. If Part 2 went from 1:10 to 2:00, that is your
  half band and he should see it.
- Second Reading passage if you have one, otherwise re-run the first for timing only — he
  will remember the answers, so mark the clock rather than the score.
- **Last fifteen minutes:** logistics. ID, arrival time, what he is allowed to bring. Then
  stop teaching.

**Homework:** nothing academic. Tell him to sleep.

---

## Day 6 — the day before

Do not teach. If he asks for a session, give him twenty minutes of light speaking so his mouth
is warm, and end early. New material the day before a test does not land, and it raises
anxiety at exactly the wrong moment.

---

## Using the Writing Marker

Paste his essay in exactly as written. Do not tidy it first — the errors are the data.

Read the **evidence** line under each band before you accept the band. The model is estimating,
and you are the one who has heard him speak and read his other work. Override any band you
disagree with; the sliders recompute the overall.

Use the **priorities** list to plan the next session, and the **rewrite** panel in the lesson —
showing him one sentence of his own made better is worth more than a general principle.

Correct three errors with him, not six. Tell him which three and why those three.

---

## When it goes wrong

**He hasn't done the homework.** Do not spend the session on it and do not lecture. Make him
do a shortened version in the first fifteen minutes while you watch. The lesson time is worth
more than the point about discipline.

**He panics about the score.** Go back to the numbers. He has evidence of improvement in
specific measurable things — time spoken, word count, reading accuracy. Show him those rather
than reassuring him, which he will not believe.

**He wants to memorise answers.** Stop it firmly. Examiners are trained to spot memorised
Part 2 answers and mark them down, and a memorised answer collapses the moment Part 3 goes
somewhere unexpected. Phrases transfer; stories do not.

**He asks what band he'll get.** Tell him you don't know, and that anyone who claims to is
guessing. Then tell him what you do know: which criterion is weakest and what would move it.
That is the honest answer and it is also the more useful one.
