Profile

I work on machine learning for real-world sensing and perception — computer vision, sensor and time-series data, and the places where those two meet. Most of my projects share a common thread: making models that don't just score well on a benchmark, but hold up when the data shifts.

That focus came out of research. Building activity-recognition systems from wearable sensors and video taught me how quickly accuracy collapses on an unseen subject. Testing crowd-counting models under synthetic rain showed exactly where a learned representation gives out. Both experiences shaped how I evaluate everything now: on data the model has never touched, in conditions that resemble the real deployment.

I also care about being able to explain a model's decision. Interpretability tools — Grad-CAM, integrated gradients, channel-importance, ablation studies — are part of my default workflow, not an afterthought.
